## Please describe your experience and knowledge working with culturally and linguistically diverse communities.

I have a strong expertise in understanding and working with culturally and
linguistically diverse (CALD) communities, developed across research, workshop
delivery, events, campaigns and advocacy over the past four years.

In my role as Community Health Promotion Officer at ACON, I have focused my
position on the support of CALD LGBTQ+ communities in Western Sydney, designing
and delivering peer-led health promotion that reaches 300+ participants a year.
In particular, I led the development and execution of Start Making Sense, a
workshop supporting LGBTQ+ Middle Eastern men to navigate their culture, queer
identity and sexual health. I identified the community need, conducted a series
of consultations to inform the content, and worked with LGBTQ+ Middle Eastern
community members to write, advertise and run the workshop using a
culturally-informed and peer-led approach. This also includes the ability to
navigate the potential conflict of participants of different faiths in the same
workshop environment, including those who were Muslim, Christian and Jewish,
which I did by identifying and escalating the risk rather than managing it
quietly, and by supporting volunteers who were personally affected. My ability
to facilitate trust with CALD communities and hold their differences in one room
resulted in a workshop built on the solidarity of culture and sexuality, and the
connection it created is sustained through a volunteer-led social group.

I have also co-led Starting Out West, a culturally responsive workshop for
multicultural and neurodiverse LGBTQ+ young adults, which reached an
intersectional priority population that had historically not accessed ACON's
services. Its success rested on understanding the access barriers for CALD
communities, being geographic distance and cultural safety in LGBTQ+ spaces, and
working with an external Western Sydney organisation to host the workshop in a
space those communities already trusted. The workshop has since expanded in both
content and locations, and that partnership has extended into other ACON
projects.

My work with CALD communities is not limited to workshops. I co-design
culturally responsive sexual health campaigns for multicultural LGBTQ+ men and
their partners, and I built ACON Westie, a multicultural LGBTQ+ community
platform in Western Sydney, from nothing to 3,205 followers. I use evidence to
decide what to run: reviewing participant demographic data, I found
multicultural community members were under-represented in our sexual health
workshops, and used academic research together with community consultation to
design a new initiative delivering sexual health messaging through visual arts
and social connection, on the evidence that CALD community members engaged more
readily through social events than an overt health framing.

Nor is it limited to community-facing work. Bringing a synthesis of community
engagement expertise and prior research experience, I have worked with
universities including UNSW, Western Sydney University and Monash University to
provide research methodology and community engagement advice for projects
investigating gay, bi+ and queer Asian men's HIV prevention needs, and Christian
LGBTQIA+ African heritage refugees' community connection. My own research
background is in this field: I lead-authored a scoping review on lateral
violence and microaggressions among LGBTQ+ people, published in the Journal of
Homosexuality, and co-authored three peer-reviewed papers on Aboriginal and
Torres Strait Islander LGBTIQ+ people's experiences of service provision,
education and belonging.

In advocacy, I contribute to HIV education and advocacy initiatives that
highlight the inequities experienced by multicultural LGBTQ+ communities, and I
have carried community voice into national policy. I facilitated a consultation
with CALD LGBTQ+ young people to inform the Australian Human Rights Commission's
National Anti-Racism Framework, and represented ACON at the HIV Youth Roundtable
for the Department of Health and Aged Care's HIV Taskforce and at the Gay Men's
Consultation for the National Action Plan for the Health and Wellbeing of
LGBTIQA+ People. Drawing on my experience working with Aboriginal and Torres
Strait Islander LGBTQ+ communities, through my internship at BlaQ Aboriginal
Corporation and as a research assistant on First Nations LGBTQ+ research at
Western Sydney University, I am also an active member of ACON's Reconciliation
Action Plan Champions.

I bring lived experience to this as well, having grown up queer and Vietnamese
in South-West Sydney. It informs how I work; it does not replace the practice.

## Please outline your experience working as a member of a team to deliver collaborative outcomes.

Almost everything I deliver is delivered with other people, and the outcomes I
am proudest of are the collaborative ones.

Most relevantly, I communicated and coordinated across 9 teams within ACON to
design and produce a resource setting out the organisation's priority
populations peer work initiatives. Nine teams meant nine sets of priorities,
nine review cycles and nine communities whose own language and lived experience
had to be reflected accurately. Through nuanced and time-efficient
communication, I was able to reconcile that into one resource that each team
recognised as representing their community properly.

Starting Out West is my clearest example of a collaborative outcome that
outlasted the project. I co-led it with ACON's Multicultural and Greater Western
Sydney Community Engagement Team, which meant working across two teams with
different remits, and then outward to an external Western Sydney organisation
for a venue. I used my interpersonal skills to navigate the diverse
personalities and skillsets within the team and bring them together, and the
same skills cultivated the partnership that gave us a safe community space to
run in. The collaboration did not end with the workshop: the partnership has
since extended into other ACON projects that have nothing to do with me, which
to me is the real test of whether a collaboration was worth anyone's time.

I am proactive in supporting other priority population initiatives across the
organisation, including the First Nations Health Program, the Women's Health
Program and the Asian Gay, Bi+, Queer Men's Project. That support ranges from
consultation on project plans to facilitating peer-led health promotion,
outreach at community events, and writing and language support for colleagues
from CALD backgrounds. I am also an active member of ACON's Reconciliation
Action Plan Champions, a cross-organisational group responsible for advocating
and supporting the implementation of Reconciliation Action Plan deliverables,
which is committee work in the same shape as supporting an advisory committee.

Team delivery also means the work that holds a program together. I recruit,
train and coordinate a volunteer workforce of 60+ peer educators delivering
workshops across Greater Sydney, and I provide administrative support for the
team including venue and equipment booking, budget processing, participant data
entry and database management, and agenda arrangement and minute-taking. My work
also brings me alongside colleagues from other disciplines, including social
workers, counsellors and nurses at our internal sexual health clinic, to make
sure community members get the right support rather than only the support my
team can offer.

Earlier, as a research assistant at Western Sydney University, I worked within a
research team across four concurrent projects and was second author on three
peer-reviewed papers, which is collaborative writing to other people's
timelines as much as my own.

## Please describe your experience using Microsoft Office and other computer applications, including how you quickly learn and adapt to new systems and software.

I have a strong proficiency across Microsoft Office and the wider set of
applications community programs run on, and I am equipped to troubleshoot
technical problems rather than wait for someone to resolve them.

I use Microsoft Word to produce a range of documents, including evaluation
reports, facilitator manuals and academic papers, which demonstrates my
adaptability across registers within one application. Microsoft Excel is
central to my work: I use spreadsheets to store and manage participant data,
keep records accurate and accessible, and analyse the effectiveness of my
programs' health promotion initiatives so that decisions about what to change
are driven by data rather than impression. I use Microsoft PowerPoint to build
presentations for internal teams and external partners, adapting how I convey
information to each audience. I work equally in the Google Workspace
equivalents, since partner organisations frequently work there.

Beyond Office, I use Google Forms and SurveyMonkey to build evaluation and
consultation instruments, Eventbrite for registration and participant
management, Zoom for online delivery, and Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop to
produce the visual material for campaigns and health promotion media.

The application most relevant to this criterion is Penelope, the client
information system I conduct participant intakes in. That work involves
sensitive personal information, so learning it thoroughly was a requirement
rather than a preference. I use it to record intakes, identify participants who
may not be suited to a peer group environment, seek advice on them, and refer
people to other services.

On learning new systems quickly, my consistent experience is that I learn them
because the work requires them. Penelope, Eventbrite and SurveyMonkey were all
picked up in service of a piece of work rather than through formal training. The
habit formed during my research assistant role, where I analysed qualitative and
quantitative data across four concurrent projects and moved between survey data,
interview transcripts and community workshop material, using whatever tool each
demanded. I expect Council's own records and corporate systems to be new to me,
and I am confident on that basis that I will learn them properly and quickly.

## Please explain how you establish and maintain close working relationships with key stakeholders.

I establish relationships by being useful to the other organisation's purposes
as well as my own, and I maintain them by following through reliably on the
small commitments so that the larger work becomes possible.

In my capacity at ACON I have developed and maintained partnerships with a wide
range of stakeholders, including Local Health Districts, multicultural and
faith-based organisations, sexual and reproductive health services, mental
health services, community organisations, universities and research bodies.
Externally this includes organisations such as Head to Health and the Western
Sydney Migrant Resource Centre. These relationships strengthen ACON's presence
in Western Sydney and give community members pathways to support that no single
organisation could provide alone.

Starting Out West is the example I would point to. We needed a venue that
multicultural LGBTQ+ young adults would actually walk into, so I approached a
Western Sydney organisation those communities already trusted. Securing the
venue was the straightforward part. What made it a relationship rather than a
transaction was that the work we delivered together was good, and the
partnership has since extended into other ACON projects. A partnership that
outlives the project that created it is the outcome I work toward.

Maintaining relationships with community members themselves is harder and
matters more. Running Start Making Sense meant holding the trust of participants
and volunteers of different faiths, including Muslim, Christian and Jewish
community members, through a period when tensions outside the room were high. I
held that trust by being clear with people about what the workshop was and was
not, taking concerns seriously the first time they were raised, escalating risks
rather than absorbing them, and supporting volunteers who had experienced
personal loss.

My stakeholder work also runs upward, to government and to the research sector.
I facilitated a consultation with CALD LGBTQ+ young people to inform the
Australian Human Rights Commission's National Anti-Racism Framework, and
represented ACON at the HIV Youth Roundtable for the Department of Health and
Aged Care's HIV Taskforce and at the Gay Men's Consultation for the National
Action Plan for the Health and Wellbeing of LGBTIQA+ People. I advise research
teams at UNSW, Western Sydney University and Monash University on research
methodology and community engagement. Representing an organisation in those
rooms depends on the same things as community work: knowing the subject,
preparing properly, and being someone people are willing to have back.

Internally, the same approach applies. Coordinating across 9 teams to build a
priority populations resource required understanding what each team needed
before asking anything of them, which is how I would approach working with a
Multicultural Advisory Committee and with Council colleagues across the
Community Development team.
