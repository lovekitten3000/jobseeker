	Introduction
Greater Western Sydney is home to a diverse and rapidly growing population, including multicultural LGBTQ+ communities who face unique challenges in accessing sexual health information and services. Cultural stigma, language barriers, and limited visibility of inclusive health messaging contribute to lower uptake of HIV prevention tools and testing among these priority populations.

Greater Western Sydney is the most multicultural region in Australia, and notably the third largest economy after Sydney and Melbourne. The NSW Health HIV Surveillance report recognises Greater Western Sydney as a key focus area in the goal of virtual elimination of HIV. When considering sexual health promotion to LGBTQ+ people in Greater Western Sydney, there is a need to ensure resources are distributed to enable this work to be culturally relevant and inclusive to ensure impact.

Recognising these gaps,Drawing on this lens of work, the ACON Westie Team co-developed the Keeping It Real 2025 campaign in partnership with community members from The West Ball, as part of its commitment to health equity and culturally responsive programming. Building on a four-year collaboration between ACON and West Ball, the campaign aimed to achieve two critical objectives:

Deliver accessible and relevant sexual health information, including HIV prevention strategies and innovative testing options such as MyTest vending machines.
Increase authentic representation of Western Sydney LGBTQ+ community members, showcasing everyday expressions of queerness within their cultural and social contexts.

Rooted in a culture-first approach, the campaign placed community voices and lived experience at the centre of every stage of its design and delivery. From shaping the visual aesthetic to selecting talent and crafting health messaging, the process was grounded in genuine collaboration and power-sharing with local communities. This intentional approach ensured the campaign did more than convey vital sexual health information - it created content that felt authentic, relatable, and culturally meaningful. By reflecting the identities, environments, and everyday realities of Western Sydney’s LGBTQ+ communities, the campaign fostered trust, representation, and deeper engagement.

Community representation was achieved by showcasing LGBTQ+ First Nations and multicultural people in Western Sydney as they exist in their everyday environments - embedded within their cultural communities and familiar locations. Campaign imagery captured authentic, relatable moments such as grocery shopping, enjoying bubble tea, or sharing meals with friends - activities that reflect the social fabric and cultural experience of Western Sydney.

The campaign was not only visually representative but also deeply collaborative. It was informed by lived experience, drawing on insights from both the ACON Westie Tteam and West Ball organisers. This process prioritised power-sharing, ensuring community members were actively involved at every stage - from ideating the campaign aesthetic and look, to scouting talent, and shaping and reviewing health messaging. A standout example of this collaboration was the photoshoot in Cabramatta, a vibrant multicultural hub in South-Western Sydney. This location choice challenged stereotypes of Western Sydney as lacking LGBTQ+ visibility and instead celebrated the thriving presence of queer communities of colour. The shoot itself was culturally sensitive and community-driven, with team members engaging shop owners in Vietnamese to secure permissions, resulting in authentic and respectful representation.

Keeping It Real launched in the lead-up to the West Ball 6 on October 11, 2025, Australia’s largest recurring ballroom event, and was activated across online platforms and outdoor advertising throughout Greater Western Sydney. The campaign achieved significant reach and engagement, reinforcing the value of culturally embedded strategies in promoting sexual health and community visibility.

 
Campaign Objectives 
The campaign objectives were to:
Promote HIV testing and prevention strategies, including PrEP and condom use.
Increase awareness of HIV testing options, such as through GPs, sexual health clinics, and MyTest machines.
Deliver HIV and sexual health messages that resonate with multicultural gay, bisexual, and queer men and their partners through codesign and authentic representation.

Campaign identity 
The name “Keeping It Real” came directly from the cultural roots of the ballroom scene and its concept of realness. In ballroom culture, “realness” refers to categories where participants compete to pass as straight or cisgender person in a heteronormative world.
For this campaign, the phrase was adapted to carry a dual meaning:

Honouring ballroom heritage by referencing the realness category, which is deeply tied to LGBTQ+ history and resilience during the HIV/AIDS crisis.
Affirming authenticity in Western Sydney, reinforcing that expressions of queerness in this regionthe area are genuine and valid - even if they differ from dominant inner-city narratives.

It also served as a call to action: encouraging people to “keep it real” by being open about sexual health, challenging stigma, and embracing cultural identity without compromise.

Evaluation Framework
This evaluation measures the campaign’s effectiveness in achieving its objectives, focusing on:
Digital performance (social media, paid ads, website analytics).
Community engagement (QR scans, outreach activities).
Cultural relevance (community feedback and staff reflections).
Operational insights (execution and partnerships).

A mixed-method approach was used:

Qualitative data: Informal feedback from stakeholders including sexual health clinics, community members, house leaders, and staff reflections.

Reach & Engagement
The Keeping it Real campaign officially launched on social media platforms on 11 September 2025, with a video reel titled “We’re here in Sydney, keeping it real”. This video highlighted the talent of the campaign as well as the core campaign images and poster suite - posted to TikTik as organic content, it received 4,427 views, 348 likes, 3 comments and 26 saves. Combined with organic engagement across Facebook and Instagram, the video received a total of 13,132 views, 580 likes, 17 comments, 63 shares and 34 saves.
Beyond the strong performance of the launch video, the Keeping It Real campaign achieved significant visibility and interaction across multiple channels. Organic content posted through the @aconwestie Instagram, TikTok and Facebook accounts generated a combined 38,936 views, 536 likes, 137 shares, and 25 comments, demonstrating consistent engagement with the campaign’s creative assets.
This is further demonstrated by ACON Westie’s Linktree engagement, with over 331 clicks through to the campaign landing page, hosted on the Ending HIV website.

Poster distribution
Campaign posters were distributed to public sexual health clinics across the Greater Western Sydney district, including Liverpool Sexual Health Clinic, Western Sydney Sexual Health Clinic, and Nepean Sexual Health Clinic. In response to feedback regarding available wall space for campaign materials, ACON prioritised ensuring prominent visibility within these facilities and supplied digital advertising assets for use on waiting room televisions, further enhancing the campaign's reach. 
In addition to public health clinics, campaign posters were distributed to ACON’s aTEST clinic, Aarows SOPV (Sex on Premises Venue) and to West Ball community members for them to share with their community spaces. Overall, 320 posters were distributed throughout the campaign period.

Recommendations
Expand community-led creative for future campaigns.Increase community codesign and collaboration partnerships for future campaigns.
Allocate budget strategically across messages for balanced reach, ensuring messages that may be new to the audience such as PrEP and Stigma increase their reach.
Maintain focus on cultural relevance for multicultural and LGBTQ+ communities.

Conclusion
The Keeping It Real 2025 campaign achieved exceptional reach and engagement across digital, outdoor, and community channels, connecting with over 440,000 individuals and generating more than 776,000 impressions over the span of 5 weeks. Central to this success was a codesign approach that authentically reflected the voices and experiences of multicultural LGBTQ+ communities in Greater Western Sydney, fostering genuine cultural resonance and most importantly, deepening community trust. 
This collaborative process was met with overwhelmingly positive feedback from community members and ballroom house leaders, who praised the campaign’s authenticity and inclusivity. The visible alignment with ballroom culture and the prioritisation of culturally relevant outreach reinforced a sense of belonging and respect, further strengthening trust between ACON and the communities it serves. 
The strategic allocation of resources and focus on culturally relevant messaging proved essential for maximising impact. The positive feedback from community members and house leaders underscores the value of collaborative, community-led creative processes in building lasting trust. 
left90112300Ultimately, Keeping It Real 2025 stands as a model for inclusive health messaging. It demonstrates that codesign with priority populations is not only effective in driving awareness and engagement, but is also vital for building and sustaining trust - making health communications truly responsive to community needs, attitudes, and aspirations.
