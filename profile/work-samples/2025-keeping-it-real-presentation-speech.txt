What is ‘Keeping It Real’?
Keeping It Real is a collaborative sexual health campaign between West Ball and ACON Westie, with support from EndingHIV
Aimed at a Western Sydney LGBTQ+ audience, with two main aims:
Delivering sexual health information to WS LGBTQ+ community
covering the basics of all HIV prevention methods with focus on access in GWS
Highlighting new health initiatives that have seem great success and uptake in GWS – such as MyTest, which are vending machines that dispense HIV self-test kits
Other aim is to increase representation of WS LGBTQ+ comm by highlighting the everyday expressions of queerness in Western Sydney – often feedback is that community members don’t feel represented within sexual health campaigns – and that’s something we wanted to address in doing something targeted towards our community
[Next Slide]
How did ‘Keeping It Real’ come to be?
I mentioned before that this was a collaboration with West Ball
For a bit of context, ballroom, which originated in NYC, is historically a place for multicultural LGBTQ+ people to create a safe space for one another
During the 80s and 90s, many of New York’s ballroom scene members were impacted by the HIV/AIDS crisis, and as a response, ballroom community leaders set up foundations for community-led HIV testing at their balls
West Ball is the first ballroom event in Australia, and is an annual event hosted in Western Sydney that attracts 300+ attendees, majority are TGD and from multicultural Bg. It is one of the largest recurring events of LGBTQ+ visibility in Western Sydney
It was West Ball who first approached to partner with ACON to carry on the legacy of community sexual health response within ballroom spaces
This was particularly relevant in the context of Western Sydney, where we’re see a divergent HIV epidemic that disproportionately impacts multicultural communities
ACON has been supporting West Ball for 4 years, and you see on screen here first collaborative campaign, which launched in 2022
KIR is continuation of the relationship that has been built over these 4 years – this second campaign aiming to reach a wider GWS audience, compared to the first campaign that targeted ballroom communities specifically

What was the process of creating ‘Keeping It Real’?
I want to start by highlighting that our work in Western Sydney is grounded in a culture-first approach
That is, we begin our health promotion processes by centring our community’s cultural identities and their connection to culture and place. From there, we explore how queerness sits within that cultural context - rather than the other way around
And so, in the context of a campaign, that means really thinking about how do we represent culture within the imagery
In the case of Keeping It Real, that was achieved through showing LGBTQ+ multicultural and First Nations people in Western Sydney existing in the everyday, amongst their cultural community and location
As you can see, the campaign images show our community going grocery shopping, getting bubble tea, going for a late night feed with friends – all very much characteristic of the Western Sydney cultural experience and social scene
This campaign was a collaborative process informed by lived experience – both from the ACON Westie team and the West Ball organisers
The process itself involved power sharing with the community, with them being actively involved in all processes of campaign creation and execution, including ideating the campaign aesthetic and look, scouting talent, and writing and reviewing health messaging
[Next Slide]
One part I wanted to highlight was the photoshoot that we did in Cabramatta
Cabramatta is a multicultural hub in SWS – a great place to challenge the narratives of Western Sydney as a homophobic space or a space without community, and really highlight that LGBTQ+ people of colour exist and thrive in Western Sydney
And as you can see with the BTS footage on screen, talents were very comfortable and were making TikToks on the day of the shoot – which was a joy to see
Our team’s lived experience also really supported the process of shooting – allowing us to communicate with shop owners in Vietnamese to ask for permission to shoot in their shops – resulting in a lot more of an authentic representation and culturally involved process
After photoshoot, went for a big feed in Cabramatta – which is a really cultural way of coming together and building trust with community

End product
So, as you already have seen, the combination of embedding lived experience, building trust, and embedding culture resulted in the creation of Keeping It Real
The tagline of Keeping It Real is a reference to the ‘realness’ categories in ballroom culture, where walkers compete to ‘pass’ as a straight and/or cisgender person
But also for us, really reinforcing the idea that Western Sydney expressions of queerness are genuine and not ‘compromising’ on identity, even if they look different to the dominant narrative in inner city gay scenes
And of course, also encouraging to “keep it real” about sexual health, and talk about it even if for some of our cultures it is a bit of a taboo

Result and impact
Over the span of 5 weeks, Keeping It Real had over 770k impression and reached almost 400k accounts. These are great numbers for what is a mini campaign on a fairly modest budget
Organic engagement with the posts was quite high, drawing on the momentum of ACON Westie and the positive support from the ballroom community
We received positive from community members that this was a campaign they could see themselves represented in
This campaign was also positively received by our stakeholders, such as the sexual health clinics and LHDs in GWS
