While there are many factors that contribute to this divergent HIV epidemic in GWS, there are two elements to highlight within the HIV sector. That is:
The lack of lived experience
The deficit narrative
[Next slide]
The first challenge is the lack of lived experience of multicultural communities in the HIV sector response
Historically, the key to Australia’s successful HIV response is the peer-led approach that draws on the lived experience of those most affected
GBQ+ men, sex workers and injecting drug users, were at the forefront of the HIV response
Yet, as the epidemic have shifted, the sector representation has not changed over the years to represent multicultural communities within the response
Lack of representation in the sector has also replicated some dynamics that we see early in the HIV epidemic, namely…
[Next slide]
The deficit narrative – which blames the individual for poor health outcomes
Often in the HIV sector, we hear multicultural communities spoken about in a way that places the blame on their culture
Those cultural communities are homophobic, so that’s why they don’t want to engage with our services
Sex is a taboo in that culture, and that’s why they lack sexual health knowledge
These statements frame ‘culture’ as a thing to be fixed and put blames on multicultural communities, rather than adapting service approaches to better fit the needs of multicultural communities
This approach also fails to recognise the strengths, resilience, cultural knowledge and assets these communities offer – and how we can draw on cultural connections and expertise to further our work
This contributes to ongoing divergent HIV outcomes, limited service reach, and distrust from multicultural communities within our sector
[Next slide]
So in addressing the issue, we need to challenge the deficit narrative and keep lived experience at the centre of our work
That means having the lived experience of people of colour and multicultural people being reflected in all levels of the workforce, including leadership and management
Within ACON Westie’s team, we ensure that our work centres culture as an enabler of health and wellbeing, working together with our communities and empowering them through partnerships, codesign and collaboration
We’ll share some case studies highlighting successful initiatives with multicultural communities in GWS

SMS ME
SMS ME is a workshop about navigating culture and queer identity for gay/bi+/queer ME men
The idea of this workshop originated from an expressed need from community members, which led to establishing a ME WG to codesign the workshop
What distinguishes this workshop is in the reframing of culture as a point of connection and healing for the participants - instead of the deficit narrative of culture as experiences of rejection, homophobia, or disconnection. Eg:
Having a ME dinner – bonding over food & food as positive connection to your culture
Talking about cultural values - marriage, having kids, rejecting Western ideas of coming out, and how sexual health interplays with that in a destigmatising and educational way
For many participants, SMS ME is the first time they’ve engaged with any LGBTQ+ group or ACON service in their local area, or at all
This workshop received the highest evaluation scores of all ACON Peer Ed workshops to date, which speaks to the real success in ensuring the work resonates with community
[Next slide]
West Ball
West Ball is an annual ballroom event in GWS that attracts 300+ attendees, majority are TGD and from multicultural Bg. It is one of the largest recurring events of LGBTQ+ visibility in Western Sydney
On screen is ‘Keeping It Real’, a co-created HIV and sexual health campaign with West Ball organisers, who approached us with the idea of doing a collaborative sexual health campaign after identifying a need for this within their community
‘Keeping It Real’ challenges the deficit narrative by featuring LGBTQ+ people of colour not only existing but thriving in Western Sydney
This collaboration was a successful campaign that reached over 130,000 accounts and nearly 400,000 views
More importantly, we received positive feedback from community members that this was a campaign that they saw themselves in
‘Keeping It Real’ demonstrates the power of challenging the deficit narrative, to instead reframe it to enable successful continuous partnerships that effectively engages multicultural LGBTQ+ communities through health promotion campaigns
[Next slide]
ACON Westie
Launched in February this year, ACON Westie is our social media platform across Instagram, TikTok and Facebook for LGBTQ+ people in GWS
It has over 1.7k followers on IG alone, and is the fastest growing ACON social media to date
Development came from an expressed gap from community → lack of representation + community visibility + information finding
Creation was led by our team, drawing from our own lived experience, alongside consultation from community members in GWS
[Next slide]
As you can see from the branding, moves away from conventional LGBTQ+ imagery, leans into the intersection of culture, queerness, Western Sydney identity → seen with the cultural logo variations
[Next slide]
Been an effective platform to increase visibility and representation, connect community, and deliver sexual health info
Since the creation of ACON Wesite, we’ve seen a high increase in engagement with our events and initiatives in GWS, as well as in sexual health-related content
Shows the effectiveness of embedding lived experience alongside creative use of social media and digital technologies for broader reach and impact of health messaging

So what do all these initiatives have in common?
All of these case studies were grounded in and informed by multicultural lived experience, and centred culture as foundation to wellbeing
They were community-led, with community-defined solutions– amplifying their agency and voice
By centring lived experience, we’ve shown ways of challenging the ‘deficit narrative’ and reimagining how the work can be done, and how we can be innovative and creative with health promotion approaches to engage with multicultural communities
Through this approach, we have created local and culturally-responsive initiatives in GWS, addressing broader health needs alongside laying foundations to improve HIV and sexual health outcomes
