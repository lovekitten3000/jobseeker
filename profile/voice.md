# Voice

How I write when I am applying for a job. This is the TONE parameter the
tailor reads. It describes the register of a cover letter, not casual writing.

## Register

Formal, in the register an Australian public-sector or university selection
panel expects. Measured and professional rather than warm or chatty. Slightly
more distant than a startup cover letter would be: no exclamation marks, no
"I'd love to", no personal anecdotes used as hooks.

Not stiff, though. Plain words over inflated ones. Say "ran" rather than
"orchestrated", "built" rather than "architected". Short sentences carry the
argument; long ones only when the idea genuinely needs the clause.

## Structure

Address the posting's **selection criteria explicitly**. NSW government, APS,
council, and university panels score against a published criteria list, and a
letter that visibly answers each criterion reads as competent to that audience.
Where a posting lists criteria, mirror its own wording as the spine of the
letter. Where it doesn't, fall back to a narrative letter that still makes one
clear argument.

Lead with the angle (see `evidence-bank.md` → `## Angles`), then prove it with
specific evidence. Every claim traces to an `ev:NNNN`.

## Australian conventions

- Australian spelling: organise, programme (for a program of work) vs program,
  centre, recognise, specialise, behaviour, licence (noun).
- "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander" or "First Nations" in full. Never
  abbreviate to initials.
- Currency in AUD.
- Dates as "August 2022", not "08/2022".

## Sector language

I work in LGBTQ+ health promotion and community engagement, and I write the
way that sector writes: "community-informed", "lived experience", "culturally
responsive", "priority populations", "co-design". These are real terms of art
in this field, not buzzwords, and a panel in this sector expects them. Use
them where they are accurate. Do not use them to paper over a gap.

## What I don't want

- Corporate filler and AI-speak. The banned list in `config.yaml` under
  `style.banned` is the hard gate; treat it as a floor, not a ceiling.
- Claiming enthusiasm as a substitute for evidence. If the argument rests on
  how much I want the role, the argument is weak.
- Writing around a gap. If I don't have something the role asks for, the
  letter states it plainly once and moves on. See `## Shortfalls`.
